## The Hardy Irony

G. H. Hardy famously claimed in 1940 that number theory had no practical applications. He celebrated this: pure mathematics was beautiful precisely because it was useless.

Thirty-seven years later, RSA encryption proved Hardy wrong. Prime numbers became the foundation of digital security. Every HTTPS connection, every encrypted message, every secure transaction depends on the difficulty of factoring large primes.

The blog didn't witness this original transformation—RSA predates it by three decades—but it tracked what came next: the world's growing awareness that number theory now underpins daily life.

A 2019 post titled "Why RSA works" explains:

> RSA works because it's easy to multiply two large primes together, but hard to factor the result. This asymmetry is the foundation of public key cryptography.

Simple statement. Deep mathematics. The characteristic approach.

## What the Crypto Posts Cover

The 2019-2025 cryptography posts form a curriculum:

**Fundamentals:** How RSA works. Why factoring is hard. What makes a prime "cryptographically useful."

**Elliptic curves:** The newer foundation for cryptography. Smaller keys, same security. Posts explain the mathematics that makes this work.

**Privacy applications:** GDPR compliance. Differential privacy. K-anonymity. The intersection of mathematics and regulation.

**Attacks and defenses:** Side-channel attacks. Timing vulnerabilities. How cryptography fails in practice.

## The Urgency

The cryptography spike reveals something about how the blog works.

Professional need creates attention. When Cook's clients needed privacy solutions, he wrote about privacy. The posts emerged from active problem-solving, not abstract curiosity.

But the posts outlasted the immediate need. The 2019 spike subsided, but cryptography remained elevated. The topic had been integrated into the blog's permanent interests.

The pattern throughout: professional exposure introduces topics, but only genuine fascination sustains them. Cryptography stuck because it connected to number theory—the primes and finite fields Cook already loved.

## The Modern Number Theory

The cryptography posts complete a loop.

Number theory began as pure mathematics—beautiful, useless, beloved. Cook wrote about it that way in 2008, exploring prime curiosities and distribution patterns.

By 2019, the same mathematics had become essential. Primes weren't just fascinating; they were infrastructure. The blog's long engagement with number theory suddenly had commercial relevance.

A reader who followed Cook's prime posts from 2008 would be prepared to understand RSA in 2019. The curriculum was already there; it just gained urgency.

## What the Spike Reveals

The cryptography spike demonstrates the blog's responsiveness. It's not an archive of fixed interests. It adapts to the world.

But the adaptation isn't arbitrary. New topics succeed when they connect to existing interests. Cryptography worked because Cook already cared about primes and number theory. Privacy worked because Cook already understood statistics.

The blog isn't everything about mathematics. It's the mathematics Cook knows, expanded when the world demands something adjacent.
