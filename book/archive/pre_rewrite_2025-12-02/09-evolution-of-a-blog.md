# Chapter 9: The Evolution of a Blog

> "I started out posting a little more than once a day on average. I've slowed down a little... My posts are getting a little longer. They're still fairly short, but a little longer than they used to be."
> — "3,000th blog post" (2019)

---

Seventeen years is long enough to see evolution.

The blog that launched in January 2008 is not the same blog that published its 5,233rd post in November 2025. Earlier chapters showed the patterns—the temporal rhythms, the topography of categories, the mathematical obsessions. This chapter asks a different question: what does the *change* mean?

---

## From Notebook to Institution

The change isn't just about volume; it's about authority.

**The Notebook Phase (2008-2012):** The early blog reads like a consultant's scratchpad. "Here's a fix for a PowerShell bug." "Here's a link to an article." The voice is helpful but tentative. It's a professional sharing notes.

**The Clarification (2013-2017):** As the professional focus shifted, the voice tightened. The "tips and tricks" vanished. The mathematical explanations grew longer. The author stopped pointing at other people's work and started synthesizing his own.

**The Authority (2018-2025):** The modern blog doesn't just explain topics; it connects them. A post about the gamma function references a post about statistical mechanics which references a post about Python code. The voice is no longer just helpful; it is authoritative. The blog has become a reference work.

The arc is recognizable: experimentation, distillation, maturity.

---

## What the Arc Reveals

This isn't just a blog getting older. It's a practice finding its form.

The early era shows someone writing about *everything*—useful observations from consulting work, mathematical curiosities, software tips, business insights. The middle era shows deliberate narrowing: what remains when professional obligations and passing interests are stripped away? Mathematics remains. The recent era shows what sustained focus produces: deeper posts, clearer explanations, the authority that comes from having explained the same territory for a decade.

The data confirms the depth. The median post length across the blog is 312 words, but the mean is 348. That 36-word gap reveals a long tail of deep-dive essays—the outliers that pull the average up. These are the posts where the blog stops being a notebook and starts being a textbook.

The 2014 dip—the quietest year—looks like a crisis from the outside. But it might have been clarification. The posts that year were fewer and shorter, and they sit on the hinge between the early notebook and the later deep dives—the calm before the length and depth of the later years arrive. Something was being decided about what the blog was *for*.

Compare a 2008 post to a 2023 post on the same topic. Consider the gamma function—a recurring obsession. In 2009, "The most important function not on a calculator" (256 words) offers a brief definition and a few applications. In 2023, "Maxwell-Boltzmann and Gamma" (635 words) derives the connection between the gamma function and statistical mechanics, includes Python code for verification, and links to three related posts. Same topic. Same author. Different voice—more confident, more substantive, more embedded in the archive's web of connections.

---

## What Changed, What Didn't

**Changed:**
- Post length doubled
- Math percentage tripled
- PowerShell, clinical trials, Perl disappeared
- Cryptography emerged as a major theme
- The professional consultant's voice faded

**Didn't change:**
- Tuesday remained peak day
- Morning remained peak time
- Monthly distribution stayed flat
- The core fascinations (pi, primes, special functions) persisted
- The characteristic voice—concise, concrete, code-showing—remained recognizable

The consistencies matter as much as the changes. They suggest something persistent underneath the evolution: a mind that works a certain way, regardless of what it's working on.

---

## The Lesson

The evolution shows: you don't need to know what you're building when you start.

The 2008 blog didn't know it would become a mathematical institution. It was a consultant's notebook with scattered interests and short observations. The purpose emerged through the work itself—through years of showing up, writing what seemed interesting, and gradually discovering what kept mattering.

This is how most sustained practices develop. The beginning is messy. The middle is clarification. The end (if there is an end) is confident focus.

The blog's seventeen-year arc is a case study in finding your subject by doing the work. Not by planning first and executing second, but by executing until the plan reveals itself.

---

## For Creators

If there's a lesson that transfers, it's this:

**Start before you're ready.** The 2008 blog wasn't polished. It was exploratory, scattered, occasionally random. That was fine. The polish came later.

**Let interests compete.** The early era's breadth—software development, math, business, PowerShell—wasn't a mistake. It was an experiment. The middle era's narrowing happened because the competition produced a winner.

**Trust the process to clarify.** The 2014 dip wasn't failure; it was focus emerging. Sometimes fewer posts means better posts. Sometimes the quiet year is the year that matters most.

**Keep showing up.** The recent era's quality didn't come from talent or luck. It came from having explained the gamma function and the Fourier transform and elliptic curves so many times that the explanations became effortless.

The blog demonstrates what sustained practice produces: not just accumulated content, but accumulated capability. The 2025 voice is better than the 2008 voice because it has more practice behind it.

---

## The Archive as Evidence

Unlike most creative development, this one is documented.

Usually, the messy early work disappears. Drafts get deleted. Early attempts get buried. The public sees only the polished result, with no trace of the stumbling that preceded it.

The blog preserves the stumbling. The short, scattered 2008 posts are still there. The PowerShell tutorials that no longer reflect current interests remain in the archive. The middle-era transition is visible in the changing ratios.

A reader who starts from the beginning witnesses seventeen years of development in compressed form. They see topics emerge and fade. They watch posts lengthen and deepen. They experience someone finding their subject.

This is rare evidence: what sustained intellectual work actually looks like, including the parts that don't fit the final shape.

---

## Looking Forward

The data ends in November 2025, but the blog presumably continues.

What will the next decade show? Will new interests emerge, or will the mathematical focus deepen further? Will AI change how the blog operates—or become a topic it explains?

Whatever comes, it will build on seventeen years of established pattern. The blog knows what it is now. Future evolution will be variation on a settled theme, not the search for identity that characterized the early years.

For readers of this book, the question isn't what Cook will do next. It's what *you* will do.

The blog proves the thesis of this book's title: you can just do things. You can write about what interests you, publish without permission, show up day after day, and let the work find its form. You don't need a plan. You don't need a platform. You need time and consistency.

What will you build with your next seventeen years?
