# Chapter 4: The Quiet Year

> "I started out posting a little more than once a day on average. I've slowed down a little."
> — "3,000th blog post" (2019)

2014 is an anomaly.

One hundred forty-seven posts—the lowest year in the archive. Average length: 188 words, also the lowest. The 11-day gap in July—the longest silence in seventeen years.

The numbers don't explain why. The blog offers no announcement, no "I'm taking a break," no explanation of reduced capacity. The posts simply came less frequently for a while.

This chapter is about what happens when a practice is tested.

---

## The Numbers

| Year | Posts | Avg Words | Change |
|------|-------|-----------|--------|
| 2012 | 357 | 216 | — |
| 2013 | 260 | 230 | -27% posts |
| 2014 | 147 | 188 | -43% posts |
| 2015 | 168 | 293 | +14% posts |
| 2016 | 209 | 386 | +24% posts |

The dip begins in 2013, bottoms in 2014, and recovers through 2015-2016. But the recovery looks different from the decline. Posts get fewer, then posts get longer. The blog that emerges from 2014 is not the same blog that entered it.

---

## The 11-Day Gap

July 9 to July 21, 2014.

This is the longest silence in the archive. Before July 9, posts appeared on July 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, and 9. After July 21, they appeared on July 22, 23, 24, 25, and 28. The gap is an island of silence in a sea of regularity.

What happened in July 2014? The data doesn't say. Vacation, illness, crisis, decision—any of these could produce an 11-day gap. The blog records the silence but not its cause.

The gap is significant for its singularity. In seventeen years, no other gap approaches it. Whatever happened in July 2014 was exceptional.

## The Career Context

In 2013, Cook left MD Anderson Cancer Center after thirteen years. He became a full-time independent consultant, founding Singular Value Consulting.

The timing aligns. The clinical trials posts stop after 2013. The blog begins to shed its professional obligations. But the transition also created uncertainty. Building a consulting practice takes time. The first year of independence is rarely the most productive.

Perhaps 2014 was the year the blog nearly died—not from abandonment, but from competition with more urgent demands.

Or perhaps the blog was being reimagined. Fewer, shorter posts because the author was deciding what the blog was *for*.

The data can't distinguish between crisis and clarification. Both look the same from above.

---

## What Survived

The core rhythm survived. Tuesday still led. Morning still dominated. The monthly distribution stayed flat.

The mathematical obsessions survived. Posts about pi, primes, and special functions continued, even if fewer.

The voice survived. The characteristic concision—short sentences, clear examples, code when needed—remained recognizable.

What disappeared:

- The scattered tips and observations
- The business advice
- The links to other people's work
- The notes from consulting engagements

The 2014 blog is leaner. Less noise. More signal. The posts that do appear are more focused.

This could be exhaustion producing only the essential. Or deliberate pruning. The data can't distinguish.

---

## The Meaning of Recovery

The recovery that began in 2015 wasn't a return to the old pattern. It was the emergence of a new one.

| Year | Posts | Avg Words |
|------|-------|-----------|
| 2014 | 147 | 188 |
| 2015 | 168 | 293 |
| 2016 | 209 | 386 |
| 2017 | 184 | 383 |

Post count increased modestly. Average length increased dramatically. The blog that came out of 2014 wrote fewer short posts and more substantial ones.

The 2008 blog averaged 206 words. The 2016 blog averaged 386 words. The length nearly doubled. But the change didn't happen gradually across eight years. It happened in the two years following the 2014 dip.

The quiet year was a hinge. Before it: a consultant's notebook. After it: something else.

---

## Crisis or Clarification?

The data supports two readings:

**Crisis:** 2014 was a difficult year. The career transition consumed energy. The blog nearly died. What recovered was whatever remained after exhaustion stripped away the non-essential.

**Clarification:** 2014 was a deliberate pause. Cook was deciding what the blog was for. The reduced output reflects reduced commitment to the old model. The recovery reflects commitment to a new model.

Both readings are plausible. Both produce the same data signature. The truth might be both: crisis that forced clarification, difficulty that produced focus.

Whatever happened internally, the external result is clear. The blog that emerged from 2014 was more focused, more substantial, and more distinctly mathematical than the blog that entered it.

---

## The Pattern for Practices

The 2014 dip suggests a pattern that might apply to any sustained practice:

**The early phase** is exploratory. Write about everything. Establish the habit. Quantity matters more than quality.

**The crisis** comes when the practice must justify itself. Why are you doing this? What is it for? The crisis can be external (career change, life event) or internal (boredom, doubt).

**The clarification** emerges from the crisis. The practice continues, but transformed. What survives is what you actually care about.

Not every practice survives the crisis. The internet is full of abandoned blogs where the 2014-equivalent never ended. Cook's did. The practice was stronger than the crisis.
