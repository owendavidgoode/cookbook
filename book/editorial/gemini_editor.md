# Editorial Pass: You Can Just Do Things

This is a strong manuscript with a clear, unique premise. It successfully avoids the trap of being a "great man" biography and instead becomes a fascinating study of *practice*. However, it currently suffers from "data repetition fatigue" and occasionally slips into the breathless reverence it claims to avoid.

Here is your full editorial pass, designed to sharpen this into a book that earns its place on the shelf.

***

## Snapshot Overview

**What’s Working:**
*   **The Concept:** A "data portrait" of a blog is a brilliant, fresh angle. It elevates a personal gift into a universal study of consistency.
*   **The "Edges" Chapter:** This is the soul of the book. By exploring the non-math topics (music, typography), you humanize the subject far better than a biography would.
*   **Chapter 6 (Cryptography):** This is your strongest narrative chapter. It has a clear arc (obscurity to urgency) and perfectly illustrates the "pure vs. applied" theme.
*   **The Voice (mostly):** The detached, analytical third-person voice works well. It feels like a nature documentary observing a rare species of intellect.

**What Isn’t:**
*   **Repetitive Awe:** You repeat the core stats (5,233 posts, 17 years, 1.8 million words) in almost every chapter intro. It loses impact. State it once, then trust the reader to remember.
*   **Chapter 9 vs. Chapter 1:** Chapter 9 ("Evolution") covers much of the same ground as Chapter 1 ("Shape"). The distinction between "what happened" (Ch 1) and "how it changed" (Ch 9) is too subtle. These need distinct hooks or structural merging.
*   **The "Unmeasurable" Melodrama:** Chapter 10 risks negating the book's premise. It apologizes too much for using data. It needs to be a *complement* to the data, not a retraction of it.
*   **Passive Constructions:** There is a tendency to use "The data reveals..." or "The table shows..." too often. Make the *subject* the actor, not the data.

**The Book’s Promise (One Sentence):**
*This book visualizes the invisible structure of a seventeen-year intellectual habit, proving that profound influence comes not from viral moments but from the quiet, relentless act of showing up.*

***

## Big-Picture Editorial Letter

**To:** The Author
**From:** Editorial
**Re:** *You Can Just Do Things*

You have a "quiet" book here, and I mean that as a compliment. In a market screaming for attention with hacks and shortcuts, this book offers a meditative look at the long game. It works because it treats John D. Cook’s blog not as a content farm, but as a geological formation—something that accumulated layer by layer.

**1. Positioning & Reader Transformation**
Your primary reader (John) will love this. But for the secondary reader (the blog fan or creative pro), you need to sharpen the *takeaway*. Currently, the book sometimes feels like a museum catalog ("Look at this, then look at this"). You need to pivot slightly more toward *inference*. Don't just show us the pattern; tell us what that pattern *costs* and what it *yields*.

**2. Structure: The Sagging Middle**
Chapters 1–3 are tight (Time, Topic, Rhythm). Chapters 4–7 (Obsessions, Languages, Crypto, Edges) are the meat.
*   **The Problem:** Chapter 8 (Interviews) and Chapter 9 (Evolution) drag. The "Interviews" chapter feels like a compilation of B-sides. It lacks the data-driven thrust of the others.
*   **The Fix:** Consider cutting Chapter 8 and weaving the best interview quotes into the relevant thematic chapters (e.g., put the VisiCalc quote in "Computing"). If you keep it, it must argue *why* interviewing is part of his "input" mechanism, not just list who he talked to.

**3. Voice: Kill the "Hush"**
You have a habit of using a "hushed" tone, like a narrator in a library.
*   *Example:* "Let that number sit for a moment." / "This is the shape of that work." / "The silence is more eloquent than any explanation."
*   *Critique:* It borders on hagiography. The data is impressive enough; you don't need to tell the reader how to feel about it. Be cooler. Be more clinical. Let the magnitude of the work speak for itself.

## Chapter-by-Chapter Notes

### **Prologue: You Can Just Do Things**
*   **Verdict:** Strong hook, but trim the reverence.
*   **Fix:** Cut "Let that number sit for a moment." It's a cliché. Just say "5,233 posts." The period does the work.
*   **Concept:** The "climax species" metaphor is excellent. Keep that.

### **Chapter 1: The Shape of Seventeen Years**
*   **Verdict:** Good foundation.
*   **Issue:** The "Pandemic Non-Event" section is interesting but feels slightly defensive.
*   **Suggestion:** Frame it not as "he ignored the world" (which sounds cold) but as "the practice is a sanctuary." The blog is where the chaos of the world is ordered into logic.

### **Chapter 2: The Topography of Interest**
*   **Verdict:** Solid. The treemap description is clear.
*   **Issue:** The list of categories in the text duplicates the table/visual.
*   **Fix:** Don't list the percentages in the text *and* the table. Use the text to narrate the *surprises* (e.g., "Clinical trials is a ghost town," "Typography is tiny but persistent").

### **Chapter 3: The Rhythm of a Working Mind**
*   **Verdict:** The "Tuesday" insight is great.
*   **Fix:** The section "The Anti-Pattern" is confusingly named. Rename to "The Invisible Schedule." The contrast between "I have no schedule" and the data proving otherwise is the strongest insight in the book—highlight it more.

### **Chapter 4: The Mathematical Obsessions**
*   **Verdict:** Good, but watch the lists.
*   **Issue:** The "Named Functions" section reads like a glossary.
*   **Fix:** Connect them more to the *why*. Why *these* functions? Because they are the "connectors." Frame the chapter around "The Connectors" rather than just a list of favorites.

### **Chapter 5: Languages as Thinking Tools**
*   **Verdict:** Functional, but dry.
*   **Opportunity:** The "Translation Layer" section is the key. Move this up. The value of the blog isn't that he knows Python; it's that he *translates* Math to Python. That's the core value proposition.

### **Chapter 6: Cryptography’s Moment**
*   **Verdict:** **The Best Chapter.**
*   **Why:** It has a plot. The world changed, and the blog was ready. Do not touch the structure of this chapter. It is perfect.

### **Chapter 7: The Edges of the Map**
*   **Verdict:** Essential for humanizing the data.
*   **Refinement:** The "Clinical Trial Mystery" is a bit dramatic ("The silence is more eloquent..."). Just state the facts: It stopped. It marks a career change. The data shows the boundary between the personal (Music, which stayed) and the professional (Trials, which left).

### **Chapter 8: The Interviews**
*   **Verdict:** **Weakest Chapter.**
*   **Problem:** It disrupts the "data portrait" flow. It feels qualitative in a quantitative book.
*   **Recommendation:** CUT. Distribute the best insights (Atiyah on examples, Bricklin on tools) into Chapter 9 (Evolution) or Chapter 4 (Obsessions). If you must keep it, rename it "Input Mechanisms" and analyze *who* he talks to as a data set (e.g., "He talks to builders, not critics").

### **Chapter 9: The Evolution of a Blog**
*   **Verdict:** Redundant with Chapter 1.
*   **Fix:** Chapter 1 is about *Volume* and *Time*. Chapter 9 should be about *Voice* and *Authority*. Focus Chapter 9 purely on the qualitative shift—how the *writing style* changed. You mention "Post length doubled"—that's a Chapter 1 stat. Focus here on the *content* shift.

### **Chapter 10: What the Numbers Miss**
*   **Verdict:** A bit apologetic.
*   **Fix:** Change the frame. Instead of "What the numbers miss" (apology), call it "The Dark Matter" (mystery). The influence is the gravity that holds the system together, even if we can't see it.
*   **Cut:** The section "The Uncountable" feels repetitive. We know we can't count debugging sessions.

### **Epilogue & Appendices**
*   **Verdict:** "The Irony" section in the Epilogue is fantastic. Keep it exactly as is. It’s honest and modern.

## Sample Line-Level Edits

**1. Stopping the "Data Narrator" Voice**
*Original (Ch 2):*
"The table reveals a story that the total count obscures. [Table] The early era produced 1,734 posts..."
*Edit:*
"The total count obscures the real story. In the early era, Cook wrote 1,734 posts..."
*Why:* Stop introducing the table. Just tell the story. The table supports you; it doesn't lead you.

**2. Removing False Drama**
*Original (Prologue):*
"Let that number sit for a moment. 5,233 posts... This is a book about what that looks like."
*Edit:*
"5,233 posts. Roughly one every 1.25 days, continuously, for seventeen years. This book visualizes that endurance."
*Why:* Respect the reader's intelligence. They know 5,000 is a big number.

**3. Sharpening the Insight**
*Original (Ch 3):*
"This is the gap between what someone says about their work and what the data shows. Cook doesn't experience himself as following a schedule."
*Edit:*
"Cook claims no schedule, but the timestamps disagree. He doesn't have a calendar; he has a metabolism."
*Why:* "Metabolism" is a stronger, more biological metaphor for an internal rhythm.

## Title & Subtitle Suggestions

**Current:** *You Can Just Do Things: How to Become Internet Famous*
**Critique:** "How to Become Internet Famous" is the wrong promise. It sounds like a TikTok guide. This book is about *quiet* influence, not fame.

**Option 1 (The Direct approach):**
*Title:* You Can Just Do Things
*Subtitle:* A Data Portrait of Seventeen Years of John D. Cook

**Option 2 (The "Practice" approach - Stronger Market Fit):**
*Title:* The Shape of Consistency
*Subtitle:* Seventeen Years, 5,000 Posts, and the Architecture of a Quiet Career

**Option 3 (The "Intellectual" approach):**
*Title:* A Climax Species
*Subtitle:* Visualizing the Life of a Mathematical Blog

**Recommendation:** Stick with *You Can Just Do Things* (it's a great "Cook-ism"), but change the subtitle to **"The Data of a Quietly Influential Life."**

## Priority Revision Checklist

1.  **Kill the Repetition:** Search for "17 years" and "5,233" and cut 50% of the instances.
2.  **Combine/Cut Chapter 8:** Decide if "Interviews" pulls its weight. If not, harvest the quotes and delete the chapter.
3.  **Refine Chapter 10:** Rename to "The Dark Matter" (or similar) and remove the apologetic tone.
4.  **Strengthen Chapter 9:** Differentiate it clearly from Chapter 1. Make it about *Quality/Authority*, not *Quantity/Time*.
5.  **Voice Check:** Read the manuscript aloud. If you sound like a PBS narrator ("Here we see the majestic..."), rewrite it to sound like an engineer ("The data shows...").
6.  **Visual References:** Ensure the text says "Figure X shows..." less often. Instead, describe the pattern: "As the heatmap shows, 2014 was a ghost town."
