# Chapter 6: The Shedding

> "Number theory has traditionally been the purest of pure mathematics. People study number theory for the joy of doing so, not to make money. At least that was largely true until the advent of public key cryptography."
> — "Surprising applications of math" (2011)

Focus requires shedding. As the blog deepened into mathematics, it left things behind.

This chapter is about what disappeared.

---

## The Clinical Trials Extinction

| Year | Clinical Trials Posts |
|------|----------------------|
| 2008 | 24 |
| 2009 | 6 |
| 2010 | 6 |
| 2011 | 2 |
| 2012 | 6 |
| 2013 | 1 |
| 2014 | 1 |
| 2015 | 5 |
| 2016 | 3 |
| 2018 | 1 |

The pattern is extinction. Two dozen posts in 2008, then a steady decline to single digits, then effectively zero in recent years.

Clinical trials was Cook's day job at MD Anderson. When he left in 2013, the topic began to fade. By 2018, it was down to a single post.

Cook spent thirteen years designing clinical trials. He has 5,881 Google Scholar citations, mostly from that work. His paper on "dose-finding based on efficacy-toxicity trade-offs" remains influential in biostatistics. He could have built an audience around clinical trial methodology.

He didn't. When he founded Singular Value Consulting in 2013 and left MD Anderson, the topic faded. Professional obligation, not personal fascination.

---

## The PowerShell Fossil Record

PowerShell tells a similar story.

Cook wrote about PowerShell consistently from 2008-2012, producing 47 posts on scripting techniques, language features, and Windows automation. This represents serious investment in mastering the tool.

After 2012, PowerShell posts decline. The last PowerShell post appears in July 2014. The tool had been replaced.

The replacement was Python. The first SciPy-tagged post appeared in February 2009. By 2025, Python had accumulated 207 tagged posts. The transition was generational: Windows automation gave way to scientific computing.

## The Business Advice Fade

Two hundred six posts carry the "Business" category. Their distribution:

| Era | Business Posts | % of Era |
|-----|---------------|----------|
| 2008-2012 | 126 | 7.3% |
| 2013-2017 | 53 | 5.5% |
| 2018-2025 | 27 | 1.1% |

The early blog gave business advice. "Task switching." "Why programmers cannot be managed." "Good, fast, or cheap: Can you really pick two?"

These read like lessons from the consulting trenches. Someone who has made mistakes, trying to save others the trouble.

The later blog doesn't give business advice. The voice shifted from consultant to mathematician. The audience shifted from professionals to learners.

## What Rose as Others Fell

The shedding wasn't just loss. It created space.

| Category | 2008-2012 | 2018-2025 | Change |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Math | 357 (21%) | 1,611 (64%) | +43 pts |
| Statistics | 205 (12%) | 120 (5%) | -7 pts |
| Computing | 145 (8%) | 314 (12%) | +4 pts |
| Business | 126 (7%) | 27 (1%) | -6 pts |
| Clinical trials | 44 (3%) | 1 (0%) | -3 pts |

Math went from about one-fifth to nearly two-thirds of output. The narrowing was dramatic.

This isn't accidental. Every hour spent on business advice was an hour not spent on number theory. Every PowerShell post was a Python post not written. The later blog's mathematical depth required abandoning breadth.

## The Programming Language Transition

| Language | 2008-2012 Posts | 2018-2025 Posts |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| PowerShell | 48 | 0 |
| Perl | 11 | 4 |
| Python | 77 | 68 |
| Mathematica | 9 | 65 |
| C++ | 33 | 1 |

PowerShell essentially went to zero, Perl nearly so. Python and Mathematica became the dominant languages in the late era.

The transition reflects a broader shift in scientific computing. In 2008, data analysis might happen in many environments: Perl scripts, PowerShell pipelines, MATLAB, Mathematica. By 2018, Python had won. NumPy, SciPy, pandas, matplotlib became the standard stack.

Cook rode this wave. His blog became a resource for Python-based mathematical computing because he shifted with the field.

## The Interviews That Stopped

Between 2009 and 2016, Cook conducted 10 interviews for the blog:

- Sir Michael Atiyah (Fields Medalist)
- Robert Ghrist (applied topology)
- Cliff Pickover (science writer)
- Dan Bricklin (VisiCalc creator)
- Rich Hickey (Clojure creator)

The interviews are among the longest posts in the archive. The Atiyah interview runs 1,926 words. They required coordination, planning, editorial judgment.

After 2016, the interviews stop. The blog became a solo practice again. Whatever energy the interviews required, it was redirected elsewhere.

---

## What Persisted

Some things didn't change:

**Pi** (153 posts) spans all seventeen years. The first pi post appeared in 2008; the most recent in 2025.

**Primes** (103 posts) similarly persist. The mathematical core remained stable while everything around it shifted.

**The Fourier transform** (45 tagged posts) appears throughout. Signal processing was never a professional obligation; it was genuine interest.

**Special functions** (140 posts) never fade. The gamma function, Bessel functions, hypergeometric functions—these define the blog's mathematical identity.

The pattern: professional topics faded. Personal fascinations persisted. The blog became a map of what Cook actually wanted to think about: freed from what he was paid to think about.

---

## The Shedding as Liberation

The 2013-2017 period was a shedding phase. Clinical trials, PowerShell, business advice, interviews—all faded.

What remained was mathematics. Pure mathematics applied to computation. Number theory made concrete through code. Statistical distributions made visible through Python.

The shedding wasn't failure, it was focus. The blog couldn't be everything to everyone. By 2018, it had discovered what it was.

---

## So What?

*Depth requires abandonment. Moving from 21% mathematics to 64% mathematics meant letting PowerShell, clinical trials, and business advice go to zero—trading consultant breadth for mathematician focus. Professional obligation produces citations; personal fascination produces seventeen years of sustained work. The blog became valuable not despite the narrowing, but because of it.*
